# Lehman Brothers Data Sources and Temporal-Admissibility Register

This file records the principal public sources used in the Lehman Brothers application of the ITI–gITI–IDI framework.

The purpose is to preserve source provenance, temporal admissibility, and the distinction between:

- principal pre-failure evidence;
- validation-only evidence;
- contextual market or event evidence;
- post-event material used only for interpretation or falsification.

The source register is governed by a strict temporal firewall.

## 1. Temporal Firewall

For any pre-failure institution-period observation:

- only evidence publicly available by the relevant cutoff may generate ITI;
- only contemporaneously available documentary evidence may generate gITI;
- later comparative disclosures may validate an earlier value but cannot create the earlier principal observation;
- post-bankruptcy examiner reports, litigation records, hearings, or retrospective analyses cannot generate pre-failure ITI or gITI;
- unresolved source gaps remain missing rather than being backfilled.

The guiding rule is:

**public after period t ≠ admissible for constructing period t**

unless the information was already publicly available by the relevant cutoff.

## 4. Unresolved Principal Source Gaps

The current strict-temporal-firewall database retains three important unresolved areas:

- 2006Q3 contemporaneous filing / earnings-release extraction;
- exact 2007Q2 exhibit-level contemporaneous numerics;
- 2007Q3 contemporaneous Form 10-Q / earnings-release extraction.

These gaps are recorded as missingness.

They are not repaired through later comparative disclosures.

## 5. Post-Event Sources

Post-event materials may include:

- bankruptcy examiner reports;
- congressional or regulatory investigations;
- litigation records;
- later academic reconstruction;
- retrospective market analysis.

These materials may be used only for:

- validation;
- interpretation;
- falsification;
- mechanism comparison.

They do not generate principal pre-failure ITI or gITI values.

## 6. Market and Event Evidence

Market evidence such as:

- stock prices;
- CDS spreads;
- bond spreads;
- rating actions;
- market capitalization;
- external funding disruptions;
- bankruptcy-event timing

may be used for:

- external comparison;
- lead / lag analysis;
- conventional-indicator benchmarking;
- robustness testing.

Such variables do not automatically enter the principal gITI.

gITI is generated from documented institutional relationships, not from a generic collection of realized market outcomes.

## 7. Source-to-Measurement Rule

A single source may support more than one analytical layer only where it contains separately defined empirical objects.

For example:

- a reported liquidity amount may enter the quantitative candidate matrix;
- a statement that the liquidity pool must absorb loss of repo capacity may support a relational gITI edge.

The quantitative amount and relational dependency remain analytically separate.

## 8. Minimum Provenance Record

Every admitted observation should retain:

- Evidence_ID;
- institution-period;
- source title;
- source type;
- public / filing date;
- SEC accession or stable archive identifier;
- URL or archival location;
- page, note, or section where available;
- extracted value or relationship;
- units;
- ITI / gITI / contextual classification;
- principal / robustness / validation-only status;
- contemporaneous-admissibility status;
- extraction or coding note.

## 9. Current Source Status

The current Lehman empirical database contains sufficient contemporaneous evidence to generate:

- the principal first-run ITI;
- the principal late-window gITI;
- aligned IDI and signed divergence;
- alternative ITI specifications;
- alternative alignment;
- conservative gITI robustness tests.

The early-period source record remains incomplete.

The database therefore supports a bounded longitudinal empirical test rather than a claim of complete reconstruction of Lehman Brothers.

## 10. Evidence Principle

The governing evidentiary rule is:

**missing evidence remains missing; later knowledge does not become earlier evidence.**

This rule is central to the credibility of the Lehman application because the framework tests what could be reconstructed from contemporaneously available information before terminal failure.
